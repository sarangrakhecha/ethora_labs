Software Engineering

Software built around your business. Not the other way around.

Most software does not fail on the day it ships. It fails eighteen months later, when the person who wrote it has gone, nobody is sure what the code does, and a small change takes three weeks. We build applications that survive that stage.

For teams carrying systems that are hard to change, integrations that keep breaking, or an idea that needs to be built properly the first time.

Discuss a software engineering project

Challenges we solve

  • The system works, but nobody wants to touch it

    Changes take longer than they should, estimates are padded with fear, and the people who understand the risky parts have left. The code is not necessarily bad — it is undocumented, untested and entangled, so every change is a gamble.

  • Two systems that should talk, don't

    Data is re-keyed by hand between tools, exports are emailed around, and a spreadsheet has quietly become part of the process. The integration was never built because it was never quite anyone's job.

  • The prototype became production

    Something built quickly to prove a point is now carrying real users and real data. It was never designed for that, and the decisions that made it fast to build are now the ones making it fragile.

  • Rebuild or repair?

    A rewrite is proposed roughly once a year and never happens, because nobody can say with confidence what it would cost or what would be lost. The question needs an honest technical answer, not an opinion.

What we do

  • Custom web applications

    Applications built for how your business actually works, rather than configured around a product that nearly fits. We favour straightforward, well-understood technology that another team could pick up.

  • Backend services and APIs

    Services with explicit contracts, sensible error handling and behaviour that stays correct when a request is retried. An API is a promise to other systems, so we design it as one.

  • System integration

    Connecting the tools you already run, including the ones with awkward or undocumented interfaces, with attention to what happens when the other side is slow, down or has changed without warning.

  • Internal business tools

    The unglamorous software that quietly runs operations. Built properly, these remove manual work permanently rather than moving it somewhere else.

  • Software architecture

    Boundaries drawn around business capabilities, decisions written down with their trade-offs, and a shape the team can hold in their heads.

  • Application modernisation

    Improving a system in place, in reviewable steps, with tests added around behaviour before it is changed — rather than a rewrite that stops all other work for a year.

  • Technical discovery and prototyping

    When the right answer is not yet obvious, a short, time-boxed investigation that produces evidence instead of a longer meeting.

Our approach

  1. 01AssessWe read the existing code, talk to the people who maintain it, and establish what is actually true today rather than what the documentation claims.
  2. 02PlanWe agree the smallest first piece that produces something real, and name the risks we expect to hit. Plans that survive contact with reality are short.
  3. 03ImplementWork arrives in reviewable increments. You see progress continuously, not at the end.
  4. 04VerifyTests are written alongside the code, and we are explicit about what is covered and what is not.
  5. 05Hand overDocumentation, a walkthrough and whatever your team needs to own it. We aim to be easy to stop working with.

This sits inside our wider Design, Build, Verify, Improve cycle.

Engagement options

  • Technical assessment

    Short, fixed scope

    We review an existing system or plan and report on risks, effort and options. Useful before committing to a rebuild or a large piece of work.

  • Defined-scope project

    Agreed outcome and timeline

    A specific thing built and delivered — an application, an integration, a modernisation step — with the scope written down before we start.

  • Ongoing engineering support

    Continuing, reviewed regularly

    Continuous work alongside your team, for products that need steady development and maintenance rather than a one-off push.
