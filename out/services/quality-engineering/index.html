Quality Engineering

Find what matters. Before it becomes a problem.

Testing everything equally costs as much as testing nothing deliberately, and tells you almost as little. The useful question is not how much you test, but whether the effort is pointed at the places where failure would actually hurt.

For teams where bugs keep reaching users, testing has grown by accretion, or nobody can answer plainly whether a build is safe to ship.

Discuss a Quality Challenge

Challenges we solve

  • Bugs keep reaching users

    Things pass internally and break in production. Usually this is not carelessness — it is that the tests cover what is easy to test rather than what is likely to break, and nobody has looked at the difference.

  • Nobody can say what is covered

    There are tests, and there is a number from a coverage tool, but no one can answer the real question: if this feature broke, would anything have told us?

  • Release decisions are made on feeling

    A release is approved because the team feels good about it, or delayed because someone is nervous. Neither is evidence, and both are hard to defend when a decision turns out wrong.

  • The regression suite has become a ritual

    A long manual pass runs before every release, takes days, and rarely finds anything. It continues because stopping feels risky, not because it is working.

What we do

  • Quality strategy

    Deciding what to test, how deeply, and when — written down and agreed, so testing effort is a deliberate choice rather than an accident of history.

  • Risk-based testing

    Weighting effort by what failure would cost and how likely it is, so the critical paths get real attention and the trivial ones stop absorbing it.

  • Functional, integration and system testing

    Checking behaviour at the level where the risk actually lives, including the seams between components where defects collect.

  • Exploratory testing

    Structured, time-boxed investigation by someone who understands the product. This is where the defects nobody wrote a test case for tend to surface.

  • Regression testing

    Keeping a dependable safety net around what already works, and keeping it small enough that people actually run it.

  • Release readiness

    A clear statement of what has been verified, what has not, and what risk is being accepted — so the decision to ship is made with eyes open.

  • Defect investigation and reporting

    Reproducible reports with the evidence attached, so engineers spend their time fixing rather than reconstructing.

  • Cross-platform quality

    Deciding which browsers, devices and configurations genuinely need coverage, based on who uses the product.

How this number is produced

exposure = impact × probability × usage = 60
priority = exposure × (1 − coverage) = 36

Deliberately crude, and shown rather than hidden. It is a way to structure a conversation about where testing effort should go — not a measurement, and not a prediction.

Our approach

  1. 01AssessWe look at the product, the existing tests and how releases happen today, and find where the real exposure is.
  2. 02PlanWe propose a strategy weighted by risk, and agree where depth is worth paying for and where it is not.
  3. 03ImplementTest design, execution and reporting, working alongside your developers rather than at arm's length from them.
  4. 04VerifyWe check the strategy against what actually escapes to production, and adjust where it was wrong.
  5. 05RefineWe feed what escapes production back into the strategy, so it keeps getting sharper instead of calcifying into a document nobody revisits.
  6. 06Hand overDocumented practice, so quality does not leave when we do.

This sits inside our wider Design, Build, Verify, Improve cycle.
